“Zebra” explores identity, transformation , and our relationships with loved ones after moving to a foreign place: Do you adapt, or hold on to who you were?

Isabel chooses to adapt. She socializes, embraces new food and culture, and absorbs everything around her — sometimes at the cost of her own sense of self.

In contrast, Leslie retreats inward. He escapes into his writing, resisting change. He feels like the zebra that escaped the zoo, dreaming of Africa — only to end up stranded on an ordinary street, awkward and trapped in traffic.

As their lives drift apart, they settle into a strange in-between: too familiar to be strangers, yet no longer the lover they once were to each other — just companions living under the same roof. And now, Isabel meets someone new, someone effortlessly charming and mysterious. What happens to the once-romantic future that Isabel and Leslie dreamed of together?

## From the director

I often think of our generation as a "lost generation"—losing the ability to love as we’re blown apart by a fragmented world and an endless overcrowding of information.

We live under extreme pressure to learn, adapt, and achieve, often chasing something without even knowing why. We are constantly filled with loss, anger, and perpetual insecurity about who we are. We become so consumed by these feelings that, too often, we forget the precious, living human beings right beside us.

How are we adapting to this new age of technology, convenience, and constantly evolving forms of connection? I don’t know. But I want to capture the confusion so many of us share—and to suggest that maybe the answer isn’t far away. Maybe it’s someone close to us, waiting to be seen.
